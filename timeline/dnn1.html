01 System Architecture — Local-First Edge Inference
The Async Engine Hack
To keep things buttery smooth without burning out the hardware, the architecture relies on a strict split. The 60fps Pygame loop runs on the main thread, acting solely as the renderer and physics simulator. A background daemon thread handles the heavy lifting, running the PyTorch CPU-only GNN. This setup totally nips any frame-drop issues in the bud. It is the ultimate local-first software solution—no cloud pings, no latency, zero internet required.

02 GNN Integration — How the "Brain" Actually Thinks
Model Predictive Control (The 9-Move Lookahead)
Every 6 frames, the background thread wakes up and samples 9 candidate moves (ranging from 0 to ±1.2 radians for sharp 70-degree banks). For each move, it mathematically projects where the jet, missiles, and flares will be 10 frames into the future using pure kinematics.

It then passes these 9 hypothetical futures to the Graph Neural Network (Interaction Network). The network spits out a predicted crash probability. The move with the lowest probability wins.
The "Lava" Borders
To stop the AI from cheating by phasing through walls, a massive mathematical penalty is baked in. A 40-pixel padding acts as a "lava zone." If a projected move touches this zone, the crash probability skyrockets to 10.0, forcing the AI to bank hard away from the concrete.
Center Gravity
When no threats are present, the GNN used to just headbutt walls because all crash probabilities were 0.0. Now, a micro-penalty (distance/800 * 0.1) is applied the further the jet drifts from the center. It acts as an instinctual tether, keeping it circling the middle.
03 Physics & Mechanics — The 1v1 Tactical Deathmatch
Real Talk on Assets: There is no 3D model. There is no high-res sprite. The "Highly Advanced AI Jet" is literally an 11-pixel cyan circle (pygame.draw.circle). The "Missile Launchers" are just grey rectangles with a dot in the center. The gameplay is 100% reliant on raw geometry and fluid math.
Weapon Systems
Homing Missiles (Red): Track the target using Proportional Navigation (math.atan2). Last for 5 seconds.
Ballistic Missiles (Magenta): Dumb-fire rockets. Require drag-to-aim via right-click.
AI Counter-Strikes: The AI isn't just surviving anymore. Every 4 seconds, it locks onto a surviving ground launcher and fires a high-speed cyan payload to blow it up.
Tactical Flares (The 270° Shield)
When the GNN's lowest calculated danger probability still exceeds 0.35, it hits the panic button. It deploys 8 equidistant flare particles (amber) in a massive 270-degree arc behind it, leaving exactly a 90-degree blind spot at the nose so it doesn't fly into its own countermeasures.
04 Visual Aesthetic — Neon CRT Vibes
Fake Glows & Particle Deques
Everything looks cinematic without needing a dedicated GPU. The "glow" effect is faked by stacking three Pygame circles on top of each other with decreasing alpha levels (30, 80, 255).

Movement feels kinetic because every object pushes its coordinates into a collections.deque buffer. The jet stores its last 20 frames, while missiles store 12, drawing a fading, tapering trail that looks exactly like burning thrust.
The Michael Bay Physics
Collisions aren't just a running = False statement anymore. Impacts trigger two pure CPU visual systems:
1. Shrapnel: 20-50 high-velocity particles that shoot out in 360 degrees and slow down using simulated air friction.
2. Shockwaves: A geometric circle that rapidly expands its radius while dropping its alpha channel, giving that heavy, concussive "oomph" to every mid-air intercept or base destruction.
05 The DNN Story — Wrapping Your Head Around The Pitch
If asked what this project actually is, the game is totally secondary. This is a Multi-Agent Graph Neural Network driving Real-Time Edge Inference.

It proves that a complex scene (jets, multiple missiles, and spreading flare clusters) can be mathematically graphed as nodes and edges. By passing those relationships through an Interaction Network locally (bypassing massive VRAM requirements), the engine can run Model Predictive Control fast enough to play a 60fps game seamlessly on a standard CPU. It is a flex in data structuring, thread management, and neuro-symbolic-adjacent constraints (mixing deep learning with hard physical boundaries).
